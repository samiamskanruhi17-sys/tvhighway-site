Foundation History & Philanthropy

Over two decades of dedication to the youth and families of Martha's Vineyard.

The Vision of Gerald DeBlois (1997)

The Har-Tru clay court training facility on Barnes Road, Oak Bluffs, founded in 1997.

In 1997, West Tisbury resident Gerald "Gerry" DeBlois recognized an unmet need across Martha's Vineyard: while summer residents enjoyed access to private tennis clubs, island children had few affordable opportunities to learn a lifelong athletic discipline.

Driven by private philanthropy and a passion for community sportsmanship, DeBlois founded the Vineyard Youth Tennis Foundation with a singular, historic promise: to provide free tennis instruction and court access to every island child from first grade through high school graduation.

Building the Barnes Road Facility (2002)

The classic shingle-style clubhouse and observation deck overlooking Har-Tru championship courts.

Initially operating out of elementary school gymnasiums in West Tisbury and Oak Bluffs, DeBlois financed the purchase of an 11-acre wooded parcel on Barnes Road, situated near the island roundabout. Construction began in 2000, and in July 2002, the world-class center officially opened to the public.

Featuring four laser-graded Har-Tru clay courts, a classic New England shingle-style clubhouse designed by architect Chuck Sullivan, and an inflatable winter bubble dome, the facility immediately became a centerpiece of island athletic life.

The Golden Championship Era (2006–2018)

Generations of island youth developed championship tennis mechanics and sportsmanship at VYT.

Under the stewardship of Executive Director Scott Smith and Head Professional Michael Hallisky, VYT trained over a thousand junior players. The foundation's rigorous instruction directly fueled the dominance of Martha's Vineyard Regional High School (MVRHS) varsity tennis teams, leading them to multiple Massachusetts state championship titles.

DeBlois contributed an estimated $12 million over twenty years to ensure no child was ever charged tuition or turned away.
